The markers

Channels that never stop. A tone or a buzz, 24 hours a day — holding a frequency open so no one else can use it. Nobody official will say what for.

LIVE 4625kHz USB UVB-76 · “The Buzzer”

A monotone buzz, ~25 tones a minute, running since the Cold War. Every so often it stops and a Russian voice reads names and numbers. Officially a channel marker. Unofficially, decades of people listening for the nights it talks.

LIVE 5448kHz USB The Pip (S30)

A short, sharp repeating pip — a Russian marker like the Buzzer's cousin. Also logged near 3756 kHz at night. Occasional voice messages break the rhythm.

Spooks & numbers

Coded traffic that was never meant for you. Strings of numbers read aloud, military broadcasts that begin with a callword and end before you can write it down.

LIVE 8992kHz USB HF-GCS · US military (EAM)

The US Air Force High Frequency Global Communications System. Long silences, then a station calls “Skyking, Skyking, do not answer” and reads an Emergency Action Message in phonetics. Also on 11175, 6739, 15016 kHz. Real, encrypted, and constant.

LIVE various Numbers stations (E/S/V series)

Voices reading digit groups in English, Russian, German — one-way messages to agents in the field, unbroken since the Cold War. Schedules and frequencies are tracked by enthusiasts; priyom.org is the log everyone keeps.

Anchors

The ones that are always there. Time signals — the sound of a machine telling you, to the second, that the world is still turning. Good for testing your receiver, good for the quiet.

LIVE 10000kHz AM WWV · NIST time signal

Fort Collins, Colorado. A tick every second and a voice reading Coordinated Universal Time each minute. Also on 2500, 5000, 15000, 20000 kHz — pick whichever band is open.

LIVE 7850kHz USB CHU · Canada time signal

Ottawa. English and French time announcements over a steady tick, with a digital burst each minute. Also on 3330 and 14670 kHz. Quietly hypnotic.

Broadcast band

Off the spy channels — the loud stuff. Late-night AM talk, worldwide radio, and the fringe broadcasts that treat the airwaves like a battleground. Some of it's unhinged. That's the point.

Gone dark

Channels that used to be here. Some went off in a single night and never came back. Left in the log on purpose — this is half of what shortwave is.

GONE DARK various HM01 · Cuban numbers

A Spanish woman's voice, then a screech of digital data — hybrid voice/OFDM messages from Cuban intelligence, hourly across sixteen frequencies. In August 2024 the transmitter suffered a catastrophic failure shortly before a scheduled broadcast. It hasn't returned.

GONE DARK ~11545kHz The Lincolnshire Poacher (E03)

The most famous numbers station ever heard: two bars of an English folk tune, then a woman reading five-figure groups — a British SIS station broadcast from Cyprus. It fell silent around 2008. People still play the recordings.
