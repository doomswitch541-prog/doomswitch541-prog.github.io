Represent / find / decide

Representation is an engineered approximation, not meaning itself.

The work moved from basic document search into the geometry and statistics underneath it: which distinctions survive projection into an embedding space, how candidate retrieval trades recall against precision, and how a classifier’s threshold converts uncertain scores into consequential decisions.

01 / RepresentEmbeddings

Embeddings are learned projections whose neighborhood structure depends on training data, pooling strategy, dimensionality, and normalization. Cosine similarity suppresses magnitude and compares direction; inner product preserves magnitude information; Euclidean distance imposes another geometry entirely. Anisotropy can collapse vectors into narrow cones, hubness can make a few items appear spuriously universal, and domain shift can destroy neighborhoods that looked coherent on benchmark data.

02 / FindRetrieval

A retrieval system is a cascade rather than one similarity query. Lexical retrieval preserves rare exact terms; dense retrieval captures paraphrase; metadata filters enforce scope; approximate-nearest-neighbor indexes trade exactness for latency; cross-encoder rerankers spend more compute on a smaller candidate set. Chunk boundaries, query expansion, deduplication, and provenance determine whether high recall becomes usable evidence or an expensive pile of semantic neighbors.

03 / DecideClassifiers

A classifier exposes a decision surface over a representation. The output score is not automatically a calibrated probability, and the argmax label is not automatically the correct action. Multi-label structure, class imbalance, asymmetric error costs, threshold selection, abstention, and distribution shift all matter. Accuracy alone can hide failure; precision-recall curves, calibration error, confusion structure, and out-of-distribution tests reveal what the boundary actually does.

A retrieval path

Six linked decisions between a source and a useful result.

Collect

Keep the source, structure, and origin intact.

Chunk

Divide by meaning without losing necessary context.

Embed

Create comparable vectors with one consistent model.

Search

Return candidates by similarity and metadata.

Rank

Prefer the passages most relevant to the question.

Ground

Carry the selected evidence into the response.

The project was a foundation: learning where retrieval can fail, what a representation can and cannot preserve, and how classification turns fuzzy language into an explicit boundary that can be tested.

Geometry / ranking / evidence

The difficult questions begin after the vector exists.

SpaceNeighborhood quality

Nearest neighbors are only meaningful relative to the embedding model and corpus. A useful audit asks whether neighborhoods remain stable under paraphrase, whether irrelevant hubs dominate many queries, whether normalization changes ranking, and whether the representation preserves the distinctions the application actually cares about.

RankHybrid retrieval

BM25 and dense similarity fail differently, which makes fusion useful. Reciprocal-rank fusion can merge result lists without pretending their raw scores share a scale; a reranker can then evaluate query-document interaction directly. The retrieval budget becomes an allocation problem across candidate count, reranking depth, latency, and context-window cost.

JudgeEvaluation

Recall@k asks whether relevant evidence entered the candidate set. Mean reciprocal rank rewards placing the first useful result early. nDCG handles graded relevance across a ranking. End-to-end answer quality adds another layer: citation entailment, faithfulness to retrieved text, unsupported synthesis, and the possibility that retrieval succeeded while generation still failed.
