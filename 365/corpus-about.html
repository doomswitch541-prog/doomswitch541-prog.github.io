iOS backup · Prompt library

Corpus

A personal archive built around what was worth keeping. Corpus began as a practical way to back up material from iOS and keep a prompt library close at hand—less a grand content platform than a tool shaped around the things its maker actually wanted and needed.

LOCAL / KEPT

The useful shape

Bring scattered material into one place.

Phone exports, saved text, media, posts, and reusable prompts naturally arrive in different formats and at different times. Corpus gave that material a home where it could be backed up, read, edited, and reused without depending on the original app to remain the permanent interface.

BACK UP

Keep an independent copy

The starting need was preservation: move useful material out of an iOS workflow and into files that could remain available on the user’s own machine.

READ

Make the archive comfortable to revisit

A backup becomes more valuable when it can be browsed as a library rather than treated as an opaque export waiting for an emergency.

ORGANIZE

Give text and media a shared home

The project grew around corpus, post, and media surfaces so related material could stay together without being flattened into one giant document.

REUSE

Keep prompts ready for actual work

The prompt library belonged inside the archive because prompts were working material: things to refine, carry between tools, and return to when they proved useful.

Not everything needs a platform. Some things need a place to live.

Corpus came from building the missing utility directly: preserve the material, keep it readable, and make the prompt library easy to return to. It was personal software in the most literal sense—software made because its owner needed it.
