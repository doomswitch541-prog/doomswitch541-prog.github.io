Motion Viz

A camera became an input surface for a live particle system. MotionViz joined hand tracking, gesture interpretation, configurable particle behavior, and a touch-capable canvas into one responsive visual instrument.

The visual pipeline

Landmarks become forces instead of buttons.

The project’s module boundaries describe the system clearly: configuration, hand tracking, particles, interface state, and orchestration remain separate. MediaPipe supplies structured hand landmarks; the canvas translates position and motion into a field that can attract, repel, steer, or reshape particles continuously.

01 / CAMERA

Frames enter the browser

The camera is not treated as recorded content. It is a live sensor surface whose frames feed a hand-landmark model and disappear into the interaction loop.

02 / LANDMARKS

MediaPipe resolves hand geometry

Tracked joints provide normalized coordinates and relationships across the palm and fingers. That turns an image into structured motion data the rest of the system can use.

03 / FIELD

Gesture changes particle dynamics

Hand position, direction, openness, and movement can become forces rather than discrete controls, giving the visual response continuity instead of a button-like on/off vocabulary.

04 / CANVAS

Particles make tracking visible

The particle layer converts inference into immediate feedback. A stable loop matters here: camera sampling, landmark updates, simulation, and rendering must stay synchronized enough to feel embodied.

The body moved. The field answered.

The interesting part was not simply recognizing a hand. MotionViz made recognition legible and playable: vision produced geometry, geometry produced forces, and forces produced a visual response that could be understood without opening a debug panel.
