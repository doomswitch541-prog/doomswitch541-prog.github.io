The Warm Layer

Composable skills, chained on demand.

Not one giant prompt — a set of skills you invoke and chain, so the model composes the right register for whoever is actually in front of it.

01 · Invocation

You call skills and chain them.

Instead of writing everything into one instruction, you invoke a skill and chain it with others. Each one carries a way of talking and a job to do; the model composes them into a single voice for the moment. The invoking and chaining run on Hermes.

invoke idiom · chain archivist · chain explorer

◇ idiom → ⌈ archivist → ✦ explorer → one voice

02 · What a skill holds

A skill can carry a whole domain.

Its idiom, its references, its way of meeting people — bundled so someone fluent in that world can speak plainly and be met there, instead of being translated into a flatter register. A persona layer can also be held in memory, so it persists across the conversation.

03 · The anchor

A free, local model does the holding.

Underneath the skills sits Nemotron — a free model that runs on the machine at no per-message cost, served through RGModelServe — capable enough to keep continuity over a long thread, shift modes when the moment changes, and anchor an agentic pipeline instead of losing the plot halfway through.

04 · Load-bearing

Warmth isn't decoration here. It holds weight.

The warm part — meeting people where they are, adapting register, not lecturing — is the layer everything else rests on. This isn't prompt engineering dressed up; it's architecture, with warmth as the beam that carries the load.
